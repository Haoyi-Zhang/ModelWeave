Observation-Stable Model Unweaving: Local Certificates and
a Two-Write Boundary
HAOYI ZHANG, Xi’an Jiaotong-Liverpool University, China
HUAIJIN RAN, Nanyang Technological University, Singapore
Selective unweaving asks which aspect applications can remain after others are removed. An inverse log
answers a different question: how to recover a particular predecessor state. We study their relationship for a
finite, typed, fixed-match trace language with grouped aspect occurrences, unary observations, and constant
overwrites. A last-occurrence normalization makes each individual observation a unate Boolean function of
retention decisions. Its non-subsumed clauses are exactly its prime implicates, yielding cardinality-minimum
local failure explanations and a precise flat-CNF size bound. Across observations, these local properties do
not imply global minimality. We characterize the language’s retention functions as conjunctions of mixed
clauses with at most two negative literals, without auxiliary aspect variables. If each cell is written at most
once, policy completion reduces to implication closure and every infeasible consistent policy has a two-fact
minimum conflict. With at most two writes per cell, completion is NP-complete, even for Boolean cells, one
event per aspect, and one forced retention and removal. Global conflicts can grow arbitrarily while all local
clauses remain ternary. Complement receipts support round trips but must be regenerated after selective
replay. A separate executable checker validates outcomes, local explanations, refutations, and minimum-policy
conflicts. An exact small oracle and 50,000 bounded cases agree on 231,071 retention queries; additional
structural checks and six source-guided projections test the stated boundaries. The proofs are mathematical
arguments, not proof-assistant mechanization, and the evidence concerns the specified trace semantics rather
than rematching or deployed model repositories.
Additional Key Words and Phrases: model transformation, selective undo, reversible computation, trace
semantics, prime implicates, proof certificates, dependency analysis
1 Introduction
A woven model records a sequence of decisions: which matches were found, which observations
justified them, and which changes their advice performed. Removing an aspect need not mean
returning to an earlier point in that sequence. Other aspects may have to remain, including aspects
that ran later or whose occurrences are interleaved with the removed aspect. The final state of
the original execution is then not enough to describe the requested result. Nor is a list of inverses
enough to say whether the retained applications still mean what they originally meant.
Consider a Boolean cell initially zero. Aspect 𝑎 writes one, aspect 𝑏 also writes one, and aspect 𝑟
observes one. Removing either writer alone preserves the observation; removing both does not. A
dependency that makes 𝑟 require the original last writer 𝑏 is unnecessarily restrictive. Now change
𝑏 to write zero and 𝑟 to observe zero. The full trace remains executable, but retaining only 𝑎 and 𝑟
fails. Removing an additional aspect can thus destroy an observation rather than merely eliminate
a dependency. These are small examples of two separate phenomena: alternative providers and
later writers that restore a condition after an earlier writer destroys it.
Both phenomena are old enough that novelty cannot lie in naming them. Aspect model unweaving
already uses traceability, inverse operations, impact analysis, and separate independent, additive,
and general cases. In particular, the general case can require rematching and reweaving, and
the additive case can distinguish individual match occurrences of one aspect [Klein et al. 2009].
Reversible event structures also account for disjunctive causes and more expressive enabling and
reversal conditions [Ulidowski et al. 2018]. A theory that merely attaches a partial order to an inverse
log would miss behavior already recognized by these accounts. The question here is instead about

1.1 A stability contract, rather than an unspecified inverse
We take the recorded match instances as fixed. Each event has an aspect tag, a finite conjunction of
unary observations, and simultaneous constant overwrites. All occurrences carrying the same tag
are retained or removed together. The remaining events execute in their original order. A retention
is valid precisely when every retained event still satisfies its recorded observations. We call this
observation-stable unweaving. A consumed value that determined a captured constant must be
observed by equality; a pure test may record its full accepting set. This distinction permits exact
reasoning about a threshold test without silently claiming that an arbitrary captured computation
may change its inputs.
This contract is deliberately narrower than repeating source-level weaving. It neither discovers a
new match that an erasure makes possible nor recomputes advice on a different input. Its attraction
is that the success condition is explicit and decidable from the trace. A refusal has the equally explicit
meaning that no retention satisfying a stated policy preserves all these recorded observations.
It does not assert that no alternative model or source-level reweaving exists. The difference is
essential in both the semantics and the source-guided examples.
The state includes a finite catalog of typed cells. Boolean presence cells encode nodes and edges
with fixed endpoints, and a static event rule preserves the invariant that a present edge has present
endpoints. Scalar cells contain finite attributes. The catalog is fixed, so allocation is represented
by changing presence rather than by generating a fresh identity. This is enough to express finite
creation, deletion, property changes, and incidence-sensitive examples, while keeping the semantic
argument separate from graph matching and name generation.
1.2 What information does the trace need?
There are two different information problems. To reverse an already executed constant overwrite,
the erased old value must either be inferred from the event’s observations or stored as a complement.
Such complements are a familiar bidirectional-transformation idea, not a new account of lossless
information recovery [Hofmann et al. 2011]. To decide a selective replay, one also needs to know
what retained events observed and the order of relevant writes. Two traces can have the same
final state, named writes, and old-value receipts, yet answer a retention query differently. We give
explicit indistinguishability pairs rather than claiming that every field of an uncompressed trace is
always necessary.
Even a valid selective replay can invalidate the original receipts. With a three-valued cell initially
zero, let 𝑎 write one and 𝑏 write two. Retaining only 𝑏 is executable. Its original receipt, however,
stores one, while the correct predecessor in the selected replay is zero. An end-state comparison
sees no difference: both end at two. We therefore distinguish a valid retained execution from a
reversible retained execution whose complements have been regenerated and checked against its
actual pre-states.
The main information result is local. For a fixed observation, group all writes by aspect, discard
writes before a forced reader-tag anchor, and keep only the last remaining write of each other
tag. Each tag then has one role: it provides an acceptable value or an unacceptable value. This
makes the observation’s Boolean retention function unate: every variable has one polarity after
compilation into clauses. Established prime-implicate reasoning then gives exact local minimum
explanations [Darwiche and Hirth 2023; Darwiche and Marquis 2002]. The trace-specific step is
the normalization that exposes this structure despite repeated, interleaved occurrences. It is not a
claim to invent Boolean explanations.

1.3 Local structure and global difficulty
The local normal form does more than support diagnostics. Every local clause has one negative
reader literal, at most one additional negative writer literal, and at least one positive repairing-writer
literal. Conversely, any conjunction of clauses of that form can be realized by a Boolean trace, using
fresh cells and the original visible aspect variables. Thus the trace language has an exact Boolean
expressivity boundary without hidden aspect decisions. Being true at the empty and full retention
is necessary, but not sufficient, for expressibility.
A second boundary concerns the number of writes to each cell. With at most one write per cell,
every nontrivial condition is a binary requirement: retaining a reader requires retaining the unique
writer. Policy completion then has a unique maximum solution, found by backward propagation
from forced removals. If a consistent policy is impossible, one forced retention and one forced
removal already constitute a minimum conflict. With at most two writes, this picture changes.
Policy completion is NP-complete even with Boolean data, one event per aspect, and just one forced
retention and one forced removal. In that same fragment every local prime has at most three literals.
Small local explanations therefore do not imply easy global policy search.
The distinction also affects the word “minimum.” A smallest set of decisions that forces one
observation to fail can be larger than a smallest set that makes the whole replay impossible. Different
completions may fail at different observations. A binary choice-tree construction amplifies this
separation: all local clauses are ternary, but a minimum global policy conflict has arbitrarily many
facts. The certificates and checker expose these quantifier differences rather than reporting one
undifferentiated kind of residual.
1.4 Claims and evidence
The technical claims concern the following connected argument. Typed events preserve graph
presence and admit complement-backed round trips. Their selective replays factor exactly into
ghost observations. Last-occurrence normalization produces each observation’s complete local
prime representation, with an exact minimum-fact explanation and a representation-qualified size
bound. The resulting clause language determines both an expressivity boundary and a sharp one-
write/two-write tractability threshold. A separate certificate semantics checks successful outcomes,
global refusals, minimum collateral removals, and cardinality-minimum conflicting subpolicies.
The implementation separates the producer, the direct finite oracle, and a checker that imports
neither the producer nor its normalization. The main campaign contains 50,000 bounded inputs
and 231,071 retention queries; every query agrees with direct replay, and each input has a checked
outcome or local certificate. Separate tests cover exact local primes, small typed updates, commuting
events, inconsistent certificates, SAT and vertex-cover constructions, and the stronger expressivity
and writer-count results. Six source-guided micro-models are reported as projections, not as thirty
public benchmarks or as executions of existing weaving tools.
These are complementary forms of evidence. Mathematical induction and reductions support
unbounded finite statements. Executable enumeration tests bounded implementations and coun-
terexamples. A separate code path reduces shared implementation assumptions but is not a proof-
assistant development or independent human review. The article’s applicability rests on the declared
trace interface, not on a case count standing in for model-management deployment.

2 Typed Captured Events and Complement-Backed Round Trips
The calculus describes an execution that has already resolved its source-level matches. This section
makes that restriction operational: the event syntax states all observations, the typing rule states
the preserved graph invariant, and the receipt rule states what an inverse may use. None of these
judgments assumes that a producer’s informal account of an aspect is trustworthy.

The base is fixed across queries. The original final model may be delivered with a trace, but it is
not an input needed by this definition. Replaying from the base gives a semantic reference point
independent of any optimized unweaving strategy. It is a baseline for correctness, not a claim that

2.2 What does an observation preserve?
An observation of 𝑥 in 𝑆 preserves membership in 𝑆, not necessarily the identity of the value. For
example, over 𝐷𝑥 = {50, 150, 200}, the test 𝑥 ≥ 100 is represented by 𝑆 = {150, 200}. Removing
a write of 200 can leave 150 without violating the test. That does not justify retaining an advice
constant whose computation depended on the exact number 200. Such an advice must instead
record 𝑆 = {200} for that consumed input. The event interface therefore distinguishes a branch
condition from an exact input dependency.
The distinction is semantic, not a compression heuristic chosen after a query. For a fixed captured
event, its sets are part of the input contract and do not expand to make a requested erasure succeed.
A trace with singleton observations and a trace with wider sets are different scientific inputs.
Comparing their answers illustrates the influence of the observation specification, but is not an
optimization that preserves one fixed specification.
The syntax admits conjunctions of unary tests, not arbitrary predicates over several changing
cells. The equality 𝑥 = 𝑦 generally cannot be expressed by such a conjunction. Recording the two
original values as singletons yields a stronger stability contract, not an equivalent encoding of
equality. This restriction is central to the local analysis: once the last retained writer of one cell is
known, one observation can be evaluated without considering another cell.

Simultaneous overwrite matters. Deleting an edge and one endpoint in one event is legal when the
edge is set to zero; there is no intermediate state in which a still-present edge dangles. Conversely,
setting an edge to one while leaving an endpoint unchanged requires a singleton observation that
this endpoint is one. Deleting a node without deleting its incident edges can be admitted only when
the unchanged edge-presence cells are observed zero. Thus creation and deletion are not treated as
untyped Boolean writes even though their data representation is Boolean.
The rule is sufficient rather than complete. An unchanged edge observed present already implies,
in every well-typed input, that both endpoints are present. The rule does not exploit that implication
when another incidence changes. Rejecting some safe events is an admission boundary; it does
not weaken the preservation theorem for admitted events. A richer type analysis could admit
more inputs, but would require its own proof and could change the exact retention language if it
introduced new dynamic conditions.

may not encode several old coordinates into a new auxiliary value.

2.7 Uniform limits of reduced trace interfaces
The positive round-trip result requires a specified event interface. To see why an interface cannot in
general be reduced to writes, snapshots, or unordered receipts, consider what a decision procedure
must distinguish. Let an abstraction 𝛼(𝐵,𝑇) be all information exposed to such a procedure. If
two admitted inputs have the same abstraction but give different validity answers to the same
named retention, no procedure using only that abstraction and retention can be correct on both
inputs. This is an indistinguishability argument, not a lower bound on the runtime of a particular
implementation.

every observation-stable retention when the observation specifications are omitted.

operational meaning.

4 Local Prime Clauses and Minimum Failure Explanations
The local formula is exact, but exactness alone does not establish minimality. This section specifies
the representation and quantifiers under which a minimum claim is valid. Its Boolean foundation
is standard prime-implicate reasoning; the source of tractability is that normalization makes each
observation unate. The distinction becomes important as soon as several observations are combined.
4.1 Prime implicates and unate functions
A non-tautological clause 𝐶 is an implicate of a Boolean function 𝐻 when 𝐻 |= 𝐶. It is prime when
no proper subclause remains an implicate. Clauses are regarded as sets of distinct literals. A CNF is
unate when a variable never occurs positively in one clause and negatively in another. Renaming
some variables to their complements makes a unate CNF positive, and hence its function monotone
increasing in the renamed variables. This monotonicity is not monotonicity in the original retention
decisions.
For the normalized observation formula, the reader and bad representative tags are negative;
good representative tags are positive. Last-occurrence normalization is what gives each other tag
exactly one of these roles. A tag can still have opposite roles in different observation formulas, so
their global conjunction need not be unate.
Lemma 4.1 (The unate antichain principle). The distinct non-subsumed clauses of a unate CNF
are exactly all prime implicates of its function.

𝑧 = 0. Increasing 𝑧 to one preserves 𝐻 by monotonicity and falsifies the whole original clause, a
contradiction. Thus all prime implicates are positive.

is false, 𝐻 is false. Some input clause 𝐶 is false, so every variable of 𝐶 lies in 𝐷, giving 𝐶 ⊆ 𝐷. An

It uses the semantic profile and the structural unateness lemma. The small oracle deliberately
takes the expensive alternative over four tags: it enumerates every potential signed clause and
checks implication directly. This offers a bounded implementation test whose logic is not merely a
duplicate of the construction.
4.3 A representation-qualified optimality result
A flat CNF here uses only the original aspect variables. It has no auxiliary variables, shared suffix
nodes, existential quantification, or alternate circuit representation. Clause count counts non-
tautological clauses, and literal count counts occurrences of signed variables across clauses.
Corollary 4.3 (Minimum flat CNF). Among flat CNFs over original aspect variables that denote
𝐻𝑜, the normalized CNF has minimum clause count and minimum total literal count. A literal-minimum
CNF is unique up to clause and literal order.
Proof. Every non-tautological clause in an equivalent CNF is an implicate of 𝐻𝑜 and contains
some prime implicate. Replace each clause by a contained prime. The replacement does not increase
either count. It preserves equivalence because 𝐻𝑜 implies each replacement, while each replacement
implies the old clause. The resulting CNF consists of local primes. Omitting any prime from the
non-subsumed unate family changes its function by Lemma 4.1; thus every normalized prime
must occur. This yields both lower bounds. Equality in total literal count permits neither an extra
nonempty clause, a proper superclause, nor a duplicate. The constant-true function has the unique
empty CNF. □

5 Expressivity, Dependency, and the Two-Write Boundary
A local observation is easy to normalize. A policy, however, constrains the conjunction of all
observations, with the same aspect variables shared across them. This section characterizes that
conjunction and identifies exactly where an implication-closure account is sufficient. It then
separates the number of writes per cell from the size of local explanations and from global search
complexity.
5.1 The exact retention language without hidden aspects
Let M2 be the class of Boolean functions expressible by conjunctions of non-tautological clauses,
each containing one or two negative literals and at least one positive literal. The empty conjunction
is permitted. Variables are the visible aspect tags, not auxiliary variables that are existentially
projected after compilation. Introducing fresh scalar cells is different: cells store model state, while
extra aspect variables would introduce new policy decisions.
Theorem 5.1 (Exact scalar expressivity). The valid-retention functions of admitted scalar
constant-overwrite traces with unary observations are exactly M2. Boolean cells suffice for the converse
construction. The same M2 upper bound holds for the declared graph-presence typing fragment.
Proof. For the upper bound, combine Theorems 3.1 and 3.3. Each local clause has the negative
reader literal and at most one negative bad-writer literal. Its variables are distinct. It has at least one
positive literal, because the admitted full retention satisfies it, whereas an all-negative clause would
be false on that retention. Thus the global conjunction is in M2. For typed graphs, Theorem 2.2
ensures that satisfied observations along actual replay preserve typing; no additional mask condition
is introduced.
For the converse, take an M2 conjunction. Allocate one fresh Boolean cell initially zero for each
clause. For a clause ¬𝑟 ∨ 𝑝1 ∨ · · · ∨ 𝑝𝑘, let the 𝑝𝑗 tags each write one to that cell, in any fixed order,
followed by an event of tag 𝑟 observing one. It is executable exactly when 𝑟 is absent or some 𝑝𝑗 is
retained. For a clause ¬𝑟 ∨ ¬𝑏 ∨ 𝑝1 ∨ · · · ∨ 𝑝𝑘, first let tag 𝑏 write one, then let the 𝑝𝑗 tags write
zero, then let 𝑟 observe zero. It is executable exactly when 𝑟 or 𝑏 is absent, or some repairing 𝑝𝑗 is
retained.
Concatenate the gadgets. Their cells are fresh, so events of one gadget cannot change another
gadget’s observations. Shared tags enforce the same retention decision in every gadget. Factoriza-
tion therefore gives exactly the conjunction. At full retention, each gadget’s last positive writer
establishes its observation, so the full trace is valid. There is one cell per clause and an event count
linear in literal count. The empty conjunction is represented by an empty trace. □

5.2 Exactly when implications describe the valid masks
A dependency graph with edge 𝑎 → 𝑏 expresses the requirement that retaining 𝑎 requires retaining
𝑏. Every family defined by such implications is closed under union and intersection. In a union, an
antecedent occurs in at least one member and brings its consequent with it. In an intersection, it
occurs in both members and its consequent occurs in both as well. This property gives a stronger
test than asking whether validity is monotone under erasure.
Two examples separate the closure requirements. For base zero, 𝑎 : 1,𝑏 : 1,𝑟 : {1}, the retentions
{𝑎,𝑟} and {𝑏,𝑟} are valid, but their intersection {𝑟} is not. Alternative writers defeat intersection
closure. For base zero, 𝑎 : 1,𝑏 : 0,𝑟 : {0}, the retentions {𝑟} and {𝑎} are valid, but their union
{𝑎,𝑟} is not. Inhibition defeats union closure. Neither valid-mask family can be exactly a fixed
conjunction of tag implications.

5.5 Why unrestricted finite policy search is hard
The preceding results might suggest that all policies reduce to closure plus a few choices among
providers. Those choices can encode a satisfiability problem. Complexity is stated for unbounded
finite inputs; the executable artifact’s admission limit of twelve tags is not an assumption of the
theorem.
Theorem 5.7 (Policy feasibility). Policy feasibility is NP-complete even for Boolean scalar cells,
one event per aspect, exactly one forced-kept aspect, and exactly one forced-removed aspect.

start with a 3-CNF formula with variables 𝑧1, . . . ,𝑧𝑞 and nonempty clauses 𝐶1, . . . ,𝐶ℎ. Use initially-
zero cells 𝑥𝑖,𝑦𝑖 for each variable and 𝑐𝑗 for each clause. Introduce optional tags 𝑝𝑖,𝑛𝑖, a reset 𝑑, and
final reader 𝑟. Their single events occur in the order
𝑝1, . . . , 𝑝𝑞, 𝑑, 𝑛1, . . . ,𝑛𝑞, 𝑟.

occurs in 𝐶𝑗 . The reset writes every 𝑥𝑖 = 0. Event 𝑛𝑖 observes 𝑥𝑖 = 0, writes 𝑦𝑖 = 1, and writes 𝑐𝑗 = 1
whenever negative literal ¬𝑧𝑖 occurs in 𝐶𝑗 . Reader 𝑟 observes every 𝑦𝑖 = 1 and every 𝑐𝑗 = 1.
The all-retained trace is valid: reset 𝑑 enables every negative event, every 𝑦𝑖 is written, and

The reduction uses the classical NP-completeness of Boolean satisfiability, in the standard

inside this event language with this policy interface. It does not claim a new general SAT lower
bound. In particular, the full original trace in the reduction is always valid, so hardness is not
hidden in input admission.
The reset is important. Without it, retaining both 𝑝𝑖 and 𝑛𝑖 would fail in the full trace, violating
the original-validity contract. With it, the full trace is a lawful input; removing the one designated
reset exposes the exclusive choice. That mechanism retains the important non-monotone behavior
rather than moving the hard problem into an invalid source trace.

¬𝑣 ∨ left(𝑣) ∨ right(𝑣).
Force the root kept and all leaves removed. A retained internal vertex needs a retained child.
Following retained children in a finite tree eventually reaches a retained leaf, contradicting the
policy. Thus all 𝑘 + 1 facts conflict.
If the root-keep fact is deleted, empty retention is a witness. If the removal of one leaf is deleted,
retain exactly that leaf and its path to the root. Every retained internal vertex then has a retained
child, and no other leaf is kept. This is a valid witness. Every proper subpolicy is contained in one
such single-fact deletion, and therefore is feasible. Hence 𝑘 + 1 is minimum cardinality, not merely
the size of one inclusion-minimal candidate. □
The construction identifies the quantifier responsible for large cores. Each internal observation
needs one of two children; the global policy forbids every root-to-leaf support path. A local clause
mentions only one parent and two children, but the refusal must account for all leaf choices. No
fixed bound on local clause width can bound the size of a global minimum policy obstruction. The
finite tests instantiate two through six leaves; the general statement is supported by the argument
above, not by extrapolating those five cases.
5.8 Collateral optimization has its own reduction
For completeness, minimum collateral deletion can be isolated from satisfiability. Take an undirected
graph with ordered vertices. For each edge {𝑢, 𝑣}, 𝑢 < 𝑣, allocate an initially-zero cell. Event
𝑣 observes zero on its incoming edge cells, and event 𝑢 writes one on its outgoing edge cells.
Immediately after each vertex event, put a reset aspect that restores its outgoing cells to zero. The
full trace is valid.
Force all resets removed and leave all vertex aspects optional. For an edge 𝑢 < 𝑣, retaining both
endpoints makes 𝑣 observe one and fail. If no edge has both endpoints retained, every retained vertex
sees zero on all incoming cells, so replay succeeds. Thus retained vertices are exactly independent
sets, and removed vertices are exactly vertex covers. Minimizing collateral vertex removals is
minimum vertex cover. This polynomial construction proves NP-hardness of that optimization task
and gives a separate small exact oracle; it is not reported as a baseline speed comparison or as a
new graph-complexity result.

6 Certificates and a Separate Executable Checker
The producer’s output should reveal which guarantee it claims. A successful execution, a minimum
local explanation, an optimal policy completion, and an infeasible policy are different propositions.
Their certificates share the same admitted trace but have different quantifiers. The checker treats
the producer, its compiled clauses, and its search history as untrusted.
Separating proof construction from checking is an established design principle, including in
proof-carrying code [Necula 1997]. The present certificate language is much narrower: it validates
finite captured-event semantics rather than machine-code safety. Its raw semantic cuts are not
DRAT derivations, and no external SAT proof checker is executed. Clause-proof checking and
trimming also do not by themselves establish minimum explanation size [Wetzler et al. 2014]. Here
minimum claims require explicit additional checks.
6.1 The admitted input and the trusted surface
The executable input gives domain sizes, designated node cells, edge triples, the base state, a tag
count, and an ordered event list. Domain members are encoded as integers; the meaning of a source
attribute’s integer codes is documented in its example. Guard maps contain explicit nonempty
value sets. Write maps contain one constant per written cell. Duplicate cells, duplicate guard values,
invalid domain members, out-of-range tags, and unexpected fields are rejected rather than silently
normalized.
The checker independently validates the graph signature and base, the static event rule, and the
full original trace. It does not accept an input merely because the producer already parsed it. Its
Python integer checks distinguish actual integers from Boolean values, which otherwise behave as
integers in some operations. Retention masks must be in range, and policy keep/remove masks
must be disjoint. These are mundane checks with semantic consequences: accepting a high bit or
duplicate write can change the proposition a packet appears to prove.
The bounded implementation admits at most 512 cells, 64 node cells, 192 edge cells, 256 values per
domain, 12 tags, and 96 events. Refutation trees have depth at most the tag count and at most 10,000
counted nodes. The command-line reader also has a 16 MiB packet or decompressed-line limit.
The structural bounds do not imply that every possible serialization fits that byte limit; oversized
packets are outside the executable admission contract. No general theorem about unbounded inputs
is inferred from these bounds.
The trusted computing base includes this checker, the interpreter, and the input bytes it is asked
to check. The mathematical proof describes the intended judgments. It is not a machine-verified
proof that the Python implementation refines those judgments. The checker uses only standard-
library imports and does not call the engine, normalizer, producer, or independent oracle. That is
code separation, not independent authorship or a claim of absence of correlated design mistakes.
6.2 Successful outcomes verify both directions

occurrence in original order. The checker starts at 𝐵, tests each retained observation, and computes
the update. At every step it requires the receipt’s occurrence index, old-value domain, and old
values to match the independently computed pre-state exactly. Singleton-inferable old values must
not appear as redundant supplied coordinates.

6.3 Raw semantic cuts
A cut is a small reason that a set of partial decisions cannot have a valid completion. It identifies
an observation (𝑖,𝑥,𝑆) and a source position 𝑗 < 𝑖, or 𝑗 = −1 for the base. Its surrounding partial
assignment (𝑃, 𝐷) must force the reader kept. If 𝑗 ≠ −1, it must force the source’s tag kept, and
that event must write an unacceptable value to 𝑥. A base source must likewise be unacceptable.
Finally every write between the source and reader whose constant belongs to 𝑆 must have its tag
forced removed.
Later bad writers need not be absent. They can replace one unacceptable value with another,
but cannot make the observation hold. A good write carrying a tag already forced kept prevents
the cut from being accepted, even if the producer intends some other occurrence of that tag to be
removed. Grouping is part of the check, not a discretionary interpretation of the proof.
Lemma 6.1 (Raw cut soundness). Every completion of an accepted raw cut’s partial assignment
fails its selected ghost observation, and hence cannot be a valid replay.
Proof. The selected bad source supplies an unacceptable value and is present by its required
fact, unless it is the base. Every later potentially repairing write is absent. Thus any later retained
write is also bad, and the last value before the forced reader remains outside 𝑆. Equation (6) is false.
Theorem 3.1 excludes successful replay. □
A cut does not require its source to be the last retained writer under every completion. That
stronger requirement would introduce unnecessary decisions about later bad writers. It needs only
a retained bad source and the absence of all later good writers. This makes cuts useful leaves of
a search proof, even though they are not always the cardinality-minimum local explanations of
Section 4.
6.4 Refutation trees and authorized cost leaves
A refutation tree has three node forms. A cut leaf contains the source and observation just described.
A split node chooses a tag unassigned by its current policy and contains both children: one forces
that tag removed and the other forces it kept. A cost leaf is allowed only when the checker has
supplied an external optional-removal budget, and the current forced optional removals already
exceed it. A producer cannot authorize a cost leaf by putting a preferred budget inside that leaf.
Theorem 6.2 (Refutation soundness and finite completeness). An accepted tree proves that
its root policy has no valid completion within its authorized budget, if any. Conversely, whenever no
such completion exists, a finite tree of this form exists.
Proof. For soundness, induct on the accepted tree. A cut leaf is sound by Lemma 6.1. At a cost
leaf, later assignments cannot undo a removal already forced, so no completion can fall within
the budget. A split partitions all completions into its removal and retention children. By induction
neither part contains an admissible completion.

6.5 Cardinality-minimum conflicting subpolicies
Let the requested policy 𝑄 be a set of signed facts. A conflicting subpolicy 𝐶 ⊆ 𝑄 is one having no
valid completion. Proving that 𝐶 is inclusion-minimal requires feasibility after deleting each fact of
𝐶. Proving it is minimum cardinality among subpolicies of 𝑄 is stronger: a different smaller conflict
may use facts outside 𝐶 but still in 𝑄.
Suppose the proposed core has 𝑘 facts. Its certificate contains a refutation of 𝐶 and a valid
retained-mask witness for every (𝑘 − 1)-fact subpolicy of 𝑄. The checker constructs the required
subpolicy set itself. It checks that each witness extends its designated subpolicy and directly replays
successfully; it rejects missing, repeated, or unrelated witnesses. These witnesses need only prove
existence of a valid mask, so they do not carry complete inverse receipts.
Theorem 6.3 (Minimum policy obstruction). An accepted core certificate proves that 𝐶 is an
infeasible subpolicy of 𝑄 and that no smaller subpolicy of 𝑄 is infeasible. Every cardinality-minimum
core has witnesses of this form in the finite semantic system.
Proof. The tree proves 𝐶 infeasible. Any subpolicy with fewer than 𝑘 facts extends inside 𝑄 to
one of size 𝑘 − 1. The certificate supplies a valid witness for that extension, which also satisfies
the smaller subpolicy. Thus no smaller conflict exists. Conversely, when 𝐶 is minimum, every
(𝑘 − 1)-fact subpolicy is feasible and has a witness. Finite refutation completeness supplies the tree
for 𝐶. □

6.7 What consistency checking cannot authenticate
A producer may alter a final value while leaving its trace unchanged; the checker rejects that
inconsistency. If an adversary instead changes the base and receipts together to form a different
internally valid trace, the checker may accept it. That is the correct result for its proposition: it has
validated the packet it received. It has not proved that the packet came from a particular repository,
real model, or earlier run.
The mutation suite includes this positive control explicitly. Its acceptance prevents a misleading
security claim that all changed inputs are rejected. Input provenance, publication identity, and
source-model correspondence require trusted acquisition or separate evidence. The source-guided
examples document their abstractions rather than relying on the semantic checker to authenticate
a published diagram.
Finally, certificates do not enlarge the language. A refusal about fixed matches does not become
a refusal about all rematchings because it has been checked. A minimum fact set does not become a
minimum graph residual because it is small. The checker reduces trust in search and data processing;
it cannot repair an unstated or incorrect semantic contract.

not be read as 50,000 nonempty inverse executions. Every input’s full trace is still executed during
admission, and the structured tests separately exercise nonempty receipts, stale values, and reversal

The campaign is consequently strong evidence against many small implementation mismatches,
but not a substitute for the round-trip proof. It also does not exhaust global minimum-policy
certificates on every generated case. Those exponential obligations are tested in the small policy
and reduction suites, where exact independent answers can be enumerated.

7.5 Typing, commuting swaps, and certificate corruption
The semantic suite enumerates 8,640 event/state combinations on two nodes and one edge. Of these,
2,130 are admitted and enabled; none produces an ill-typed post-state. This tests the sufficient typing
rule against all states and event choices in that small test family. It does not establish completeness
of the rule for safe graph updates.
Thirty-two admitted independent swaps produce no difference in final state or corresponding
complements. Named counterexamples exercise replacement, inhibition, grouped repeated writes,
accepting-set versus equality observations, stale complements, order omission, and original idempo-
tent deltas. Across eleven named inputs, all 124 masks and all 675 partial policies are checked. The
local/global example confirms local size three versus global size two, including valid smaller-policy
witnesses.
The checker rejects nineteen classes of inconsistent mutation: incorrect final state or old value;
missing or duplicate old coordinates; wrong occurrence or missing receipt; Boolean or out-of-range
masks; unknown fields or duplicate writes; out-of-domain values or invalid original guards; a
nonforcing local explanation; missing minimum-core witnesses; an unforced bad source; a wrong
cost; a missing proof branch; an unauthorized cost leaf; and a dangling base edge. A changed base
and consistently changed receipt are deliberately accepted. That positive control limits the claim to
internal consistency, not provenance authentication or rejection of every changed byte.

7.7 Six source-guided projections, with visible abstraction limits
Six compact encodings cover five source figures or passages. An independent class-model addition
projects one example of aspect unweaving into node, edge, and attribute cells. An additive example
groups two match occurrences under one aspect tag: removing the aspect that supplied one match
while forcing the whole later aspect to remain is infeasible in this grouped policy. The source’s
match-level unweaving can retain the unaffected match; our stronger grouping is not reported as a
defect of the source algorithm [Klein et al. 2009].
A rematching example explicitly contrasts outcomes. Under fixed recorded matches, removing
a deletion leaves one formerly hidden match untouched. Re-running source matching would
transform that newly exposed match too. The encoded fixed-match state is intentionally different.
Two threshold projections choose previous values 50 and 150 around the source’s threshold 100
after a write of 200; those two old values are our finite representatives, not two additional published
benchmarks. One policy fails and the other succeeds because membership in the accepting set
changes.
The sixth projection encodes only the disjunctive enabling pattern from an event-structure
example: either of two earlier providers enables a later event. It fixes one order and does not
implement the source’s entire reverse-transition semantics [Ulidowski et al. 2018]. Across these
encodings all 28 masks agree with direct replay; four selected policies have checked optimal
outcomes and two have checked minimum-conflict refusals. No published tool is executed.
These projections offer concrete interpretations and deliberate boundary cases. They do not
establish production relevance, full source-model equivalence, or coverage of thirty independent
published examples. Exact input JSON, source locations, code-value explanations, and abstraction
boundaries are included so computational replay can be separated from evaluation of the literature
interpretation.

8 Relationship to Existing Interfaces and Scope
The relevant comparison is not whether another system uses a trace, a complement, a dependency,
or a certificate. Those mechanisms have extensive histories. The comparison is the proposition
that each interface preserves, and whether a result about that proposition transfers to grouped,
fixed-match retention decisions.
8.1 Unweaving and the granularity of retained behavior
Aspect model unweaving provides the historical operational connection: a model’s construction
trace supports inverse operations and impact-sensitive undo, while some situations require new
matching and reweaving [Klein et al. 2009]. Our input is instead the closed sequence after matching.
We preserve its explicit observations, not equivalence to every re-execution of a source weaver. The
source-guided rematching and additive examples make the distinction observable. Consequently,
we do not attribute inverse tracing, value-sensitive impact, or independent/additive/general case
analysis to the present work as new.
Grouping is another visible choice. A source-level aspect can have several match instances, and an
application may prefer to remove some while keeping others. Our default policy deliberately keeps
or removes all occurrences with one tag. Refining a tag into occurrence-specific tags changes the
retention domain and can make an impossible grouped policy feasible. Theorems remain statements
about the chosen input tags; a tool must not silently change their granularity to make a refusal
disappear.
8.2 Bidirectional transformations and complement information
Lens laws organize relationships between a source and a view and specify how updates propa-
gate [Foster et al. 2007]. Symmetric lenses make complement information explicit [Hofmann et al.
2011]. Our receipt-backed inverse uses the same basic idea of retaining information destroyed by a
forward map. The coordinate necessity argument is correspondingly restricted to a product storage
model, and is not a new general theory of least complements.
Relational lenses also place constraints such as functional dependencies inside a typed update
interface [Bohannon et al. 2006]. Our graph-presence invariant is much narrower. It ensures
endpoint presence for a fixed catalog but does not solve relational view-update policy or infer
all consequences of a schema. The exact selective-replay question additionally fixes an ordered

8.3 Causality, explanation, and representation
Reversible event structures already accommodate enabling relations richer than one fixed prerequi-
site per event [Ulidowski et al. 2018]. Axiomatic accounts also separate the assumptions needed for
different reversible-computation properties [Lanese et al. 2024]. Our union/intersection criterion
concerns the narrow representation by binary implications over retained tags; its failure is not a
refutation of richer causal or event-structure representations. The footprint diamond is likewise
one conservative sufficient condition, not a complete general causal semantics.
A recent reversible-logging preprint studies closure-preserving compression and rate–distortion
with an explicit adequacy assumption for logged observables [Xu 2026]. Its optimization objective
is not minimum retained-policy facts. In particular, Boolean encoding and closure reasoning can
represent signed or negative enabling conditions; the existence of an inhibiting writer here does not
by itself show that a closure-based encoding is impossible. Our impossibility result is specifically
about tag-to-tag implication families, with union and intersection counterexamples.
Prime-implicate representations and their size tradeoffs belong to knowledge compilation [Dar-
wiche and Marquis 2002]. Compatible prime reasons and distinctions between subset-minimal
and shortest explanations belong to rigorous Boolean explanation [Darwiche and Hirth 2023;
Darwiche and Ji 2022]. The last-writer normalization instantiates those ideas for one observation,
and the exact M2 characterization and write-count boundary describe the resulting trace language.
The article claims neither the invention of the prime-explanation principle nor a fastest general
explanation algorithm.
The global results explain why the distinction matters. Local polarity can change from one
observation to another. Their conjunction can therefore acquire new resolvents and shorter global
explanations, or encode hard policy choices. A local minimum certificate is a useful precise diag-
nostic, but it is not a replacement for a global satisfiability or optimum certificate.
8.4 Limits of the result and evidence
The language excludes unrestricted imperative transformations, implicit reads, external side effects,
fresh-name generation, arbitrary multi-cell predicates, rematching, and arbitrary semantic equiva-
lence of models. An observation set is part of the fixed contract, not a post hoc relaxation. Graph
updates are typed only for fixed endpoint presence; multiplicities, reachability, method semantics,
and application-specific schema invariants require additional rules.
The complexity threshold counts writer occurrences per cell while allowing large event footprints.
The local optimality bound concerns flat CNFs without auxiliary aspect variables. Minimum local
facts, minimum conflicting subpolicies, and minimum collateral removals have distinct objective
functions and quantifier universes. The checker is separately implemented but not mechanically
verified, and finite tests cannot rule out all correlated bugs or establish a general proof-assistant
theorem.
The evidence is bounded and predominantly synthetic. Only six source-guided projections are
included, and they cover five passages rather than a broad published workload collection. Empty
or unused-tag masks account for a substantial part of successful campaign certificates, as reported

8.6 Conclusion
For grouped captured events, selective unweaving is not determined by final values or inverse logs
alone. Explicit observations and ordered writes admit an exact local normalization: each observation
has a unate prime-clause family and a minimum forcing explanation in a stated representation.
Those local guarantees do not collapse the global policy problem. The language realizes exactly a
mixed one/two-negative clause class; one write per cell yields implication closure, while two writes
already yield NP-complete feasibility and arbitrarily large global conflicts despite ternary local
clauses. Complement rebasing and separate certificate checks make the retained execution’s round
trip an explicit obligation. These conclusions apply to the defined observation-stable semantics,
with the mathematical and finite evidence supporting different parts of that claim.
